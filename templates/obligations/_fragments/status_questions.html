It replaces a checklist of named steps and a row of lifecycle buttons, and the reason is not simplification for its own sake: almost every filing in this product is done by one person in one sitting on a government portal, and a four-stage maker-checker rail asked them to narrate work they had already finished. What the register actually needs back is the acknowledgement number — the row an assessment is defended with — and, when there is no number yet, why not. So: one question, two answers, and each answer asks only for what it needs. A "no" records an explanation and changes no state. That is deliberate and it is the thing most easily "improved" into a bug — an obligation somebody has explained is still owed, still dated, and still goes overdue on schedule. Deferring is what exists for a decision to postpone, and it suppresses the row precisely because somebody took responsibility for that. {% endcomment %}
