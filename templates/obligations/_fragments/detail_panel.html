Re-rendered on its own after every transition, so it is self-contained and carries the id the swap targets. `aria-live` is on the wrapper because a state change here is the whole point of the interaction and a screen reader would otherwise miss it entirely. The action list is filtered by permission on the server. That is a courtesy for rendering, not the security boundary — `apply_transition` re-checks the permission, the guards and the legality of the move, because a button being absent from a page does not make its URL absent from the network. One named action per state rather than a menu of every legal move: a solo user's next step is never a choice among several equally-plausible targets, it is the one thing that follows from where this already is.
